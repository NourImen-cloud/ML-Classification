WHAT IS CLASSIFICATION?
DATA TO DECISIONS

Data: recorded representations of something in the real world (links, words, duration).

Information: data interpreted in context that helps answer a question ("Is this email dangerous?").

Classification is just teaching computers how to turn raw data into actionable categories.

(Unlike traditional programming where you handwrite 10,000 brittle IF statements, ML algorithms learn the rules from past examples)

PROBLEM TYPES

TYPES OF PREDICTIVE TASKS IN THE WILD

Binary Classification

Output belongs to exactly one of two discrete buckets.

$y \in \{\text{Spam}, \text{Not Spam}\}$ or $\{0, 1\}$

Multiclass Classification

Output is assigned to exactly one of $K > 2$ categories.

$y \in \{\text{Primary}, \text{Social}, \text{Promotions}\}$

Multilabel Classification

An observation can possess multiple labels at once.

$Y = \{\text{Urgent}, \text{Finance}, \text{Invoice}\}$

THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION: CLASSIFICATION VS. REGRESSION

Classification predicts a discrete qualitative label: $f: X \rightarrow C$.
Regression predicts a continuous numerical value: $f: X \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ (e.g. house price or rainfall in mm).

MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION

We formally represent a supervised learning dataset as a set of $n$ labeled pairs:

$$D = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}, \quad x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d, \quad y_i \in C$$

Feature Matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$

Each row represents an observation (one email). Each column represents a measurable feature (link count, word count, sender domain).

$$x = \begin{bmatrix} \text{Links} \\ \text{Suspicious Words} \end{bmatrix}$$

Ground-Truth Label $y$ & Prediction $\hat{y}$

The goal is to learn a hypothesis function $f: X \rightarrow C$ such that predicted class $\hat{y} = f(x)$ matches true class $y$ with high probability on unseen future data.

(At prediction time, the model only receives $x$ — it has never seen $y$. If your model cheats and peeks at $y$, that's data leakage!)

HOW CLASSIFIERS DECIDE
THE FEATURE SPACE

Every observation is plotted as a point in geometric space $\mathbb{R}^d$.

Consider our 10-email running dataset:
• Horizontal axis ($x_1$): Number of links
• Vertical axis ($x_2$): Count of suspicious words

NOTICE POINT E10 (THE NOISE POINT)

Email 10 has only 2 links and 2 words, but it is labeled Spam! Real datasets always contain noise, mislabels, and overlapping distributions.

2D FEATURE SPACE MAP
DECISION BOUNDARIES

A decision boundary is a geometric surface that partitions the feature space into regions assigned to different classes.

01.

Linear Boundaries (Hyperplanes)

Logistic Regression and Linear SVM slice the feature space with a straight line in 2D, a flat plane in 3D, and a hyperplane in $\mathbb{R}^d$. Equation: $w^T x + b = 0$.

02.

Axis-Aligned Rectilinear Steps

Decision Trees test one feature at a time ($x_1 \ge 3$), creating orthogonal staircase cuts parallel to coordinate axes.

03.

Flexible Voronoi Tessellations

KNN forms complex irregular territorial boundaries based on localized neighborhood voting.

(A boundary is NOT a physical line inside the computer memory; it is the geometric threshold where $P(\text{class}) = 0.50$)

THE CORE ALGORITHMS
K-NEAREST NEIGHBORS (KNN)

💡 "Show me your friends, and I will tell you who you are." (The Cafeteria Principle)

THE 3-STEP PIPELINE

1. Lazy Storage: Zero optimization during training; memorizes all coordinate points.
2. Euclidean Distance: When new point $p$ arrives, compute distance to all stored $q$:

$$d(p, q) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d (p_i - q_i)^2}$$

3. Majority Vote: Pick $K$ nearest points; mode wins: $\hat{y} = \text{mode}(y_1, \dots, y_K)$.

THE CHOICE OF K MATTERS DEEPLY

• Small K (K = 1): High variance (Overfitting). Point E10 creates an isolated spam island in legitimate territory!

• Large K (K = 9+): High bias (Underfitting). Over-smoothed; dominant class drowns out real local clusters.

⚠️ MANDATORY RULE: FEATURE SCALING

If Links range 0–10 and Account Age ranges 0–10,000, distance ignores links! Always standardize first: $z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$.

DECISION TREES

💡 "A Sequential Game of 20 Questions or Medical Triage" (If pulse > 120 and BP < 90 → Intensive Care)

Tree Anatomy & Cuts

• Root Node: Topmost feature test.
• Internal Nodes: Subsequent partitions.
• Leaves: Final predicted class.

Geometry: Always axis-aligned box cuts (perpendicular cuts; no smooth diagonals).

Demystifying Gini Impurity

Measures disorder/chaos in a subset:

$$\text{Gini} = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^C p_k^2$$

• Gini = 0.0: Pure leaf (100% spam).
• Gini = 0.5: Maximum chaos (50/50 toss-up).
Algorithm picks split with largest Gini drop!

The Overfitting Threat

Unconstrained trees keep branching until every sample sits in its own leaf (100% train accuracy, fails on new emails).

Remedy: Constrain with `max_depth`, `min_samples_split`, or post-pruning!

(A deep decision tree is like a student who memorizes textbook page numbers by heart, but fails when questions are rephrased)

LOGISTIC REGRESSION

💡 "The Calibrated Loan Officer" (Outputs true probabilities: "82% chance of repayment" before taking action)

THE 2-STAGE ENGINE

1. Linear Score: Weighted combination across features ($z \in [-\infty, +\infty]$):

$$z = w_0 + w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + \dots$$

2. Sigmoid Squasher: Maps score smoothly into $[0, 1]$ probability:

$$\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$$

$z=0 \implies P=0.5$ (decision boundary line). Predict Spam if $P \ge 0.5$.

WHY IS IT CALLED "REGRESSION"?

Because it mathematically performs linear regression on the log-odds (logit):

$$\ln\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right) = w^T x + b$$

It regresses continuous log-odds, and classifies via threshold cutoff!

GEOMETRY: FLAT HYPERPLANE

Boundary is a strictly straight line in 2D ($z=0$). Cannot separate circular clusters without manual polynomial features.

ALGORITHM COMPARISON & SELECTION

No Free Lunch: Every algorithm trades off speed, interpretability, and boundary flexibility.

ALGORITHM
INTUITION, BOUNDARY & KEY TRADE-OFFS
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)
Cafeteria table vote. Irregular Voronoi islands. Zero training time. Slow prediction on large data. Scaling is mandatory!
Decision Trees
Game of 20 Questions. Axis-aligned orthogonal box cuts. Extremely explainable. Invariant to scaling. Prone to memorization without depth limits.
Logistic Regression
Calibrated risk dial. Flat straight hyperplane. Fast inference, calibrated probabilities. Cannot learn non-linear boundaries natively.
Support Vector Machine (SVM)
Widest safety highway. Maximum separation margin. Border points are Support Vectors. Kernel trick lifts 2D non-linear points into 3D space.

THE CARDINAL SIN

NEVER evaluate a model using the same data it was trained on!

1. Training Set (70%)

Used strictly by the learning algorithm to adjust parameters and discover boundaries.

2. Validation Set (15%)

Used to tune hyperparameters (selecting optimal $K$, tree depth, and decision threshold $t$).

3. Test Set (15%)

Locked in a vault until the very end. Measures genuine generalization on fresh unseen data.

(Testing on training data is like giving students the exact exam questions and answers the night before, and then celebrating that everyone scored 100%!)

THE GENERALIZATION GAP

Underfitting (High Bias)

Model is too simple to capture real underlying patterns.

Symptom: High training error AND high test error.

Optimal Fit (Sweet Spot)

Captures generalizable structure while ignoring random noise.

Symptom: Low test error with minimal gap to training error.

Overfitting (High Variance)

Model memorizes exact training quirks and outlier noise.

Symptom: 0% training error, but catastrophic test failure!

HOW TO TAME OVERFITTING ON THE TRAIL

1. Gather more diverse training samples  |  2. Regularization ($L_1 / L_2$ penalties)  |  3. Constrain complexity (limit tree depth, increase $K$)  |  4. K-Fold Cross-Validation.

EVALUATION METRICS

Accuracy

$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}$$

Overall percentage of correct predictions across all classes.

Precision ("Quality")

$$\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$$

When the model screams "SPAM", how often is it actually right?

Recall / Sensitivity ("Quantity")

$$\text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$$

Out of all actual spam out there, what percentage did we catch?

F1-Score

$$F_1 = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$

The harmonic mean balancing precision and recall without distortion.

I LIED.

Accuracy is completely misleading.

...on imbalanced datasets, high accuracy is a dangerous illusion.

THE 1,000-EMAIL FRAUD TRAP

Suppose a stream of 1,000 emails contains only 10 spam messages (1%).

A completely useless "dummy" model that predicts "NOT SPAM" for every single email achieves:
• Accuracy: 99.0% 🎉
• Spam Caught (Recall): 0.0% 💀

DECISION THRESHOLDS

The model gives a probability $P(\text{Spam} \mid x)$. YOU choose the threshold $t$!

High Threshold ($t = 0.90$)

"Only mark spam if 90% certain."
• High Precision: Very few False Positives.
• Lower Recall: Some spam will slip into inbox.

Ideal for spam filtering and fraud blocks.

Low Threshold ($t = 0.30$)

"Mark positive even if only mild suspicion."
• High Recall: Catches nearly all cases.
• Lower Precision: More False Positives.

Ideal for rare disease screening and security alarms.

(Precision and Recall are on a playground seesaw: pushing one up naturally pushes the other down)

CAPSTONE SCENARIO

CAMPUS EMAIL SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE CHALLENGE

Q1.

Which metric should the university president optimize?

Answer: High Precision! A student missing their scholarship notification or exam timetable because of a False Positive is intolerable.

Q2.

How should the dataset be split?

Answer: Stratified time-based split. Train on past months, test on the future month to prevent temporal leakage.

Q3.

Spammers start replacing words with emojis and images. What happened?

Answer: Concept drift / covariate shift! The trained model's feature distribution no longer reflects reality. Retraining is mandatory.

TRAIL RULES OF THUMB
01.

Inspect your feature space first

Plot your data in 2D or 3D. Check for class overlap, outlier noise (like point E10), and skewed distributions before picking an algorithm.

02.

Scale features for distance-based models

KNN and SVM will break if features have different units. Standardize features, but fit scalers ONLY on training folds.

03.

Pick the right metric for the business cost

Never rely solely on Accuracy. Evaluate Precision, Recall, and the Confusion Matrix based on real-world error costs.

04.

Treat threshold selection as a first-class engineering decision

0.5 is just a mathematical default. Tune $t$ to balance false alarms versus missed threats.
